Why this archive is unusually valuable

Not all content is equal for search and for AI. This kind is close to ideal.

People type questions. AI systems answer questions and quote sources that are already shaped as answers, attributed to a named expert. You have 54 real questions from real riders, answered at length, by name, on the record. That is the shape both systems reward, and it is genuinely hard to fake.

The plan

Ordered by what returns most per hour of work. Nothing here needs a new system, it is all on top of what exists.

  • First
    Put the transcript on the page A cleaned, chaptered, readable version of the conversation under the video. Speaker names, timestamps that jump the player. This single change turns a blank page into 12,000 words of expert content. It also makes the replay useful to the people who could not attend and will not watch 76 minutes. Frank already asked to forward the transcript to a friend.
  • First
    The basics that are simply missing Sitemap entries, a real meta description, Open Graph tags so the link looks right when shared, and a canonical. About an hour of work for the whole archive, and without it nothing else in this list gets found.
  • First
    VideoObject and FAQPage structured data VideoObject with chapters gets you a video result with key moments. FAQPage is the format most often lifted into AI answers, because it hands the model a clean question and a clean answer with a source. This is the concrete answer to the AIO question. Models quote structured, attributed Q&A far more readily than prose.
  • Then
    Products, placed where they are already being discussed Not a generic block at the bottom. A card next to the relevant chapter: the footstrap chapter gets the footstraps, the mast-position chapter gets the book, the Tarifa chapter gets the Tarifa camp, the gear chapters get video coaching. You already answer these questions for free. The product is the obvious next step for someone who just listened to eight minutes on it, and right now there is no link at all.
  • Then
    Rethink the email gate Today the whole replay is behind an email form. That protects the list but hides the content from everyone who could bring you new people. Suggested split: the transcript and chapters are open and indexable, the video stays gated. You keep the capture, and you stop being invisible. See the decision below.
  • Later
    One page per question Pull the strongest 20 or 30 answers out into their own pages under something like /wingfoil-answers/, each linking back to the replay it came from and forward to the next live session. This is where the long tail lives. "How far into the footstraps" is a search nobody else is answering with a named World Cup level coach on video.
  • Later
    Feed the other channels from the same transcript Each session already contains several Reels, a newsletter, and blog posts. The Palao discussion, the mast-position debate, the footstrap test with your son. One recording, many surfaces. The content machine already publishes weekly. Its input problem is solved by an archive it is not currently reading.

The one real decision

Gate or open

Right now a replay page gives a stranger nothing until they hand over an email. That is good for the list and bad for growth: nobody links to it, nobody finds it, and no AI can quote it.

My recommendation: open the text, gate the video. The transcript, chapters and product links are public and indexable. The recording still asks for an email. The list keeps growing from people who want to watch, and the page starts earning traffic from people who do not yet know you exist.

The counter-argument is real: fewer emails per visitor. But the current page reaches almost nobody, so a smaller share of a much larger number is still more.
